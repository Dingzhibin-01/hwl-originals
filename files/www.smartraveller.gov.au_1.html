What’s a consular case? A consular case is when assistance is provided by Australian officials to an Australian who has encountered difficulties overseas. For example, if they’ve been in an accident, a victim of crime, or arrested – this is called consular assistance. When consular officials assist an Australian, they open a case in DFAT’s Consular Information System. Every case is different. Depending on a person’s circumstance, a case can be simple or complex. Some cases are quick to resolve. Others can take months or even years. 2024-25 at a glance: ## 26,266 cases of consular and crisis assistance. ## 1,500 active consular cases on average, at any one time. ## 61,534 phone calls to the Consular Emergency Centre. That’s 1 call every 9 minutes. Australians, or their family and friends, can call the Consular Emergency Centre from anywhere in the world for advice or assistance. The phone number to call is: +61 2 6261 3305 (from overseas) 1300 555 135 (in Australia) The Consular Services Charter Our Consular Services Charter describes what we may do to help Australians overseas. It also explains situations where assistance may be limited and what we ask of Australians. Visit smartraveller.gov.au/charter for more. Consular Services Consular State of Play 2024-25 CRISIS RESPONSE # 2024-25 Some international crises and emergencies require an exceptional response to help Australians, permanent residents and their immediate family members overseas. In 2024-25, we provided assistance in 17,095 crisis cases in total . Our crisis response teams worked with local authorities and assisted Australians to leave crisis-affected areas. We also provided crisis updates and travel advice. Where a crisis occurs, DFAT can set up a crisis registration portal. This allows you or your loved ones to register your location and contact details with us so we can get in contact and provide advice. Consular Services Consular State of Play 2024-25 JAPAN 2024-25 at a glance: ## 315 consular cases, compared to 299 in 2023-24 ## 910,640 Australians travelled 1 to Japan ## 88.3% increase in travellers compared to 2018-19 Japan was the third most popular travel destination for Aussies in 2024-25, overtaking the USA. 18% of cases in Japan during 2024-25 were arrests. Japan has strict policing and laws. Police can stop you on the street, demand identification and search you and your belongings. They can also detain you for up to 23 days with no formal charge. Remember, local laws can be different Differing laws for common activites can often catch travellers out. For example, in Japan it’s illegal to: • buy or drink alcohol if you’re under 20 years old • drive with any alcohol in your bloodstream, or be a passenger in a car when the driver has been drinking • smoke on the streets in some cities. Always learn about local laws that could affect you before you travel. Top 3 case types WELFARE 91 DEATH 63 ARREST 57 > 1 Australian Bureau of Statistics.
